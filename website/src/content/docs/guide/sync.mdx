---
title: Sync
description: Your whole library on every device, through your own cloud
---

Sync usually means someone else's server: an account, a subscription, a company that can read your library. bae syncs through **your** cloud, storage you already have, and runs no server at all. Your devices write encrypted changes to that storage and read each other's; the provider holds the bytes and cannot read them.

One library, every device, and the only account involved is one you already own.

## The cloud home

A library's cloud home is one location in your storage: a folder in your Dropbox or OneDrive, your iCloud, or any S3-compatible bucket (AWS, Backblaze B2, Cloudflare R2, Wasabi, ...). It holds everything the library syncs: the library's changes, artwork, and the audio of releases you move to the cloud.

Set it up in Settings, Library, with **Set up sync...**:

1. Pick a provider.
   - **Dropbox** and **OneDrive** sign in through your browser, then ask which folder the library lives in. On Dropbox that is a shared folder, so you can invite your household to it; if you have none yet, share one in Dropbox and choose **Look Again**. On OneDrive it is any folder, and you can create a new one there. The sign-in is held by coven; bae never sees its tokens.
   - **iCloud** uses the Apple Account the device is signed in to.
   - **S3** asks for the bucket, region, endpoint, and access key, and nothing else. **Advanced** holds an optional key prefix and the service profile, automatic by default; for a service bae doesn't recognize, **Detect Settings** tests the bucket with your key and shows what it found. If the setup is refused, bae says why.
2. bae starts syncing.

A library with no cloud home is complete on its own. Local-only is a mode, not a limitation, and disconnecting the cloud home returns to it.

## Always encrypted

Every cloud home holds the same thing: encrypted objects under random names. Every change, image, and audio file is encrypted on your device before upload. Your albums are not a folder anyone can browse in your Dropbox, and the provider sees no album, artist, or file name. See [Encryption](/reference/encryption).

## What syncs

- The library: albums, releases, tracks, credits, works, and artists. Every device gets all of it, including releases whose audio stays on the device that imported them.
- People: each person's name and avatar, ratings, play history, and playlists. Your history and each of your playlists can be private to you or shared with the household.
- Artwork: covers, artist images, and avatars, fetched eagerly so grids render everywhere.
- Audio: only for releases you moved to the cloud. A local release shows on every device but plays only on the device that imported it, until you move it to the cloud. See [Storage and offline](/guide/storage).

Per-device state does not sync: playback position, volume, queue, and device settings stay on the device.

## When it syncs

Sync runs on its own: after changes, on a timer, and on demand with **Sync Now**. Edits land on other devices seconds later under normal conditions. Two devices editing at the same time both win where they don't collide: bae merges concurrent edits field by field, the later edit wins a field both changed, and a deleted release stays deleted even if it was edited elsewhere at the same moment. Nothing ever asks you to resolve a conflict.

## When it fails

Sync failures don't interrupt the library; you keep browsing, playing, and importing locally while bae retries. Settings, Library shows one line for sync: **Synced**, or how many items are waiting, such as **Waiting: 3 items**. Open it to see each item in plain words, with the action you can take: **Sign In** when the provider wants you to sign in again, putting back a file that moved before it uploaded, **Retry**, **Reconnect**, **Resume Uploads**, or **Update App**. A wait you can't help, such as for another device to come online, offers nothing. This list is the one place to look; uploads can also be paused and inspected in the [Storage Manager](/guide/storage#the-storage-manager).
